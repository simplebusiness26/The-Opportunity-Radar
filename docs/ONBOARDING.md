# Getting Radar to a useful state

The setup path is derived from your data, not from a stored "wizard completed"
flag. Delete your last capability and that step becomes incomplete again, which
is correct — the checklist is always true.

`/onboarding` shows it, and the dashboard carries a banner naming the single
next thing worth doing until the essentials are done.

## The order, and why

**1. Record what you can already build.** First, deliberately. Until Radar knows
this, every opportunity looks exactly as it would to a stranger, and the whole
point of the product is that it does not. Name each capability in your own
words; it is resolved against a shared vocabulary and anything that will not
resolve is recorded as a gap rather than mapped to a guess.

**2. Record what you can reuse.** A component you already have turns a
three-month build into a three-week one, and that difference changes the ranking
more than any market signal does. Reuse readiness matters: a concept and a
drop-in component are not the same thing at the same maturity.

**3. Say what you actually have to spend.** Days and money. Allocation answers
"where should the next unit of effort go"; without a unit, it has nothing to
allocate and will say so.

**4. Say what the next few months are for.** Wanting revenue quickly and wanting
the largest long-term market lead to different answers, and Radar will not pick
between them for you.

**5. Record some evidence.** Even a handful of observations entered by hand is
enough to run the entire loop. Name the source when there is no URL — two
entries naming the same source count as one, which is what keeps hand-entered
evidence honest.

**6. Frame your first opportunity.** The thesis the evidence is tested against.
Scoring, investigation and allocation all hang off it.

**7. Connect an AI provider** *(optional)*. Adds investigation, the red team and
Ask Radar answering in prose. Set a monthly budget first; the degradation ladder
is only meaningful against a limit that exists.

**8. Connect a source** *(optional)*. This is what makes Radar run on its own
rather than being fed by hand.

## What "good enough to trust" looks like

- More than one capability and at least one reusable asset, or leverage is
  guesswork.
- Time and budget recorded, or nothing can be allocated.
- Evidence from more than one origin per opportunity, or confidence is capped
  and Radar will keep telling you so.

## The first week

Record what you have. Record ten pieces of evidence about one problem you
already care about. Frame it as an opportunity, score it, and read the gaps
rather than the number — the gaps are where the product earns its keep.

Then, if the score is low, believe it. That is the outcome that costs you
nothing.
