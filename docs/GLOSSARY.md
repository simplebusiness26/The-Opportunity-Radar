# Glossary

The words Radar uses, and what each one is careful *not* to mean.

**Signal.** One raw observation. A signal is not evidence of anything on its
own; twenty signals saying the same thing are one piece of evidence seen twenty
times.

**Evidence unit.** A distinct claim about the world, backed by one or more
signals. This is the unit that scores.

**Mention.** One signal's contribution to an evidence unit. Counted and shown,
but it never feeds confidence.

**Independent source.** A genuinely separate origin — a different registrable
domain, a different author, a different affiliation. This is the number that
matters, and the one hardest to inflate.

**The triple.** `412 mentions · 37 unique evidence · 9 independent sources`.
Shown wherever evidence is shown. It is the product's credibility claim written
as three numbers.

**Cluster (shown as *problem*).** Evidence about the same underlying problem,
grouped. A cluster is not an opportunity; it is a thing that might be one.

**Opportunity.** A thesis about what to do, which the evidence is tested
against. It has a lifecycle and a state machine.

**Attractiveness.** How good this would be if it is true.

**Confidence.** How much of it we actually know. Computed separately and never
folded into attractiveness. It is capped below certainty, because no forecast
about the future is certain.

**Insufficient evidence.** A dimension nothing has established. Not zero. "No
evidence they will pay" and "evidence they will not pay" are opposite findings
and Radar refuses to average them.

**Fit.** How well this suits *this* team specifically.

**Leverage.** How much cheaper this is for us than for a stranger, because of
what we already have. Always a range.

**Value of information.** What answering an unknown would change, against what
answering it would cost. It is what ranks the next research action, so cheap
questions that change the decision beat expensive ones that are merely
interesting.

**Red team.** The role whose job is to kill the idea if it can be killed. Its
verdict is only allowed to be *fatal* if an objection cites real evidence.

**Counter-evidence.** Evidence attached as arguing *against* a thesis. It
reduces confidence directly, which is how red-teaming moves numbers rather than
producing prose.

**Re-evaluation trigger.** A recorded predicate describing what would change our
mind about a rejection. Evaluated deterministically against new evidence.

**Calibration.** How well Radar's own past estimates held up, computed from this
team's completed work. Refuses to adjust anything below eight samples.

**Execution Brief.** The document that crosses the boundary between deciding and
building. Assembled entirely from records; no prose is generated.

**Handoff.** A brief committed to be built, snapshotted so later edits cannot
change what the builder was given.

**Manual mode / AI-assisted / autonomous.** Derived from what is connected,
never declared. See `docs/MODES.md`.

**DEMO.** Seeded sample data. Badged wherever it appears, without exception.
